Machine Learning · Classification
AllLife Bank — Personal Loan
Campaign Optimization
UT Austin · AI & ML Program · Decision Tree Analysis
The Business Problem

AllLife Bank wanted to convert its liability (deposit) customers into personal loan customers. With a historically low conversion rate of ~9%, the marketing team needed a smarter way to identify who to target — rather than blasting the entire customer base with campaigns that mostly missed.

My Approach
  • Performed thorough EDA to understand customer demographics, financial behavior, and existing product usage patterns
  • Analyzed class imbalance in the target variable and assessed its implications on model performance metrics
  • Built and compared Decision Tree classifiers using both pre-pruning (hyperparameter tuning via GridSearchCV) and post-pruning (cost-complexity pruning) strategies
  • Evaluated models using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC — with business context guiding metric prioritization (recall matters more when false negatives are costly)
  • Identified the most predictive features driving loan conversion: income, credit card spending, education level, and CD account ownership
Key Findings
Top Predictor

Income and CC average spend were the strongest signals — high earners with active spending were disproportionately likely to convert.

Model Performance

Post-pruned tree achieved strong recall on the minority (loan = yes) class while maintaining generalizability on unseen data.

Class Imbalance

With only ~9% positive cases, standard accuracy was misleading — the project emphasized precision-recall tradeoffs for the business context.

Business Impact

Model recommendations could allow the bank to focus campaign spend on the ~20% of customers responsible for the majority of conversions.

🎯

Business Recommendation

Target customers with income above $100K, higher CC spending, and existing CD accounts first. A tiered outreach strategy using model scores could improve campaign ROI by reducing wasted spend on low-probability customers.

FoodHub — Restaurant Demand
& Customer Behavior Analysis
UT Austin · AI & ML Program · Exploratory Data Analysis
The Business Problem

FoodHub, a food delivery aggregator in New York, was looking to understand demand patterns across its restaurant network. The goal: use order-level data to uncover which restaurants, cuisines, and timeframes drive the most volume — and identify friction points in the customer experience (particularly delivery time and ratings).

My Approach
  • Framed the analysis around 6 stakeholder-defined questions before touching the data — ensuring findings would directly answer business needs
  • Cleaned and explored an order-level dataset covering cuisine type, order value, delivery time, day of week, and customer ratings
  • Used univariate and multivariate analysis to identify demand concentration, delivery time distributions, and rating correlations
  • Visualized patterns across weekday vs. weekend, cuisine categories, and price tiers to surface actionable segmentation insights
  • Translated statistical findings into plain-language business recommendations without losing analytical rigor
Key Findings
Demand Concentration

A small subset of restaurants generated a disproportionate share of orders — classic long-tail distribution with 20% of restaurants driving ~60% of volume.

Weekend vs. Weekday

Weekend demand skewed toward American and Italian cuisine; weekday patterns favored faster, lighter options — suggesting different promotional strategies per segment.

Delivery Time & Ratings

Orders exceeding 60 minutes showed a measurable dip in ratings — a quantifiable threshold for service level targets and restaurant accountability metrics.

Order Value Patterns

Higher-value orders were more likely on weekends and from specific cuisine types — pointing to upsell and partnership prioritization opportunities.

📊

Business Recommendation

Focus promotional partnerships on top-performing cuisines by day segment. Introduce a 60-minute delivery SLA with restaurant-level accountability tied to ratings — the data shows it's the clearest lever for customer satisfaction improvement.
